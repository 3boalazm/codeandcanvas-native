Creative Strategy in the Age of Generative AI

Generative tools made producing options almost free. That is exactly why choosing well became the expensive part — and why strategy, not output volume, is the real differentiator.

Published in Arabic and English · standalone Arabic page.

Creative Strategy · Code & Canvas ILLUSTRATIVE ARTWORK · NOT EVIDENCE

The abundance problem

A few years ago, producing twenty visual directions took a design team a week. Today they can be generated in minutes. But the decision did not get easier — it got harder, because the quantity of options adds no information about which one is right. Abundance hides the missing criterion.

When the criterion is not written down, the most beautiful option on the screen wins. And beauty isolated from positioning, market and production constraints is the riskiest choice: it looks convincing in a deck, then fails the first time it is applied to a small pack, a long Arabic name, or an interface full of messy data.

Generative AI raises the price of the question and lowers the price of the answer.

What cannot be generated

Four responsibilities stay human in any serious project, regardless of output quality.

  1. Defining the problem

    Which decision must change? Who is the actual audience? Which constraint is non-negotiable? A model answers a question; it does not choose the question.

  2. Judging fit

    Fit needs context: the organization's history, competitors, language and the team's ability to operate the result. That context is not in the prompt.

  3. Accountability

    When an application fails at the press or breaches a regulatory requirement, the model does not carry the consequence. The project needs a named decision owner.

  4. Rights and privacy

    Reference material, client data, font and image licences, and the model provider's policy all need documented approval before any external tool touches them.

A working method

We neither reject the tools nor build the work on them. We place them at a defined stage of the process, with stated inputs and outputs.

BEFORE GENERATION

Write the criterion

"By what will we judge an option?"

A one-page position, production constraints, an audience, and a "what we do not do" list. No tool is opened without this page.

DURING GENERATION

Diverge, then constrain

"Which options reveal possibilities we had not considered?"

Tools are used to explore breadth, not to pick a final. Every option is recorded against the criterion, not against the reviewer's taste.

AFTER GENERATION

Test in reality

"Does it survive the smallest size and the longest name?"

The final application is built by hand and tested on hard cases: RTL, empty data, print and minimum size.

Numbers and proof

It is tempting to attach a percentage about time saved. We will not do that before a dated baseline, a measurement method and publication approval. The rule we apply to ourselves is the one we apply to clients: any number without a source is rewritten or removed. Our public register states the status.

The practical conclusion: use the tools to lower the cost of exploration, and invest the savings in verification. The real gain comes not from generation speed but from fewer wrong decisions reaching production.
